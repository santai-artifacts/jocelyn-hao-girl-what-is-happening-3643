Beyond the Ivy League

Together, these twenty institutions represent the full breadth of American academic ambition: land-grant universities, research powerhouses, liberal arts strongholds, and urban centers of innovation.

History

Nearly Four Centuries of Founding

America's elite universities were founded across 276 years — each reflecting the era, values, and ambitions of its time.

1636

Harvard University Founded

The first institution of higher education in the American colonies, established by the Massachusetts Bay Colony and named after its first benefactor, Reverend John Harvard.

1701

Yale University Founded

Chartered in Connecticut as the Collegiate School, later renamed after Elihu Yale whose gift helped fund the college's first permanent home in New Haven.

1746

Princeton University Founded

Originally the College of New Jersey, the fourth college established in colonial America and a center of Presbyterian learning that became a secular research powerhouse.

1754

Columbia University Founded

Founded as King's College by royal charter of King George II in lower Manhattan — today the oldest university in New York City.

1755

University of Pennsylvania Founded

Ben Franklin's university, founded with a uniquely practical mission — to prepare students for lives in business and public service, not just the clergy.

1764–1769

Brown & Dartmouth Founded

Brown (1764) broke ground as the first U.S. college to accept students regardless of religious affiliation. Dartmouth (1769) was chartered to educate Native American youth in New Hampshire.

1789

Georgetown University Founded

The oldest Jesuit university in the United States, founded in Washington D.C. the same year the U.S. Constitution was ratified — making it a witness to the entire American experiment.

1838–1842

Duke & Notre Dame Founded

Duke (originally Trinity College, 1838) grew into a research powerhouse in North Carolina. Notre Dame (1842) was founded by French missionaries in Indiana to become America's most recognized Catholic university.

1851

Northwestern University Founded

Established to serve the then-Northwest Territory, Northwestern set up its campus on the Lake Michigan shoreline in Evanston, Illinois, building one of the nation's strongest journalism and law programs.

1853

Washington University in St. Louis Founded

Founded as Eliot Seminary (named after T.S. Eliot's grandfather), WashU grew into a premier research university in the heart of the country, known for medicine, law, and engineering.

1861

MIT Founded & Cornell Founded (1865)

MIT (1861) was chartered in response to the industrialization of America, pioneering the research university model for STEM. Cornell (1865) followed on a land-grant vision: "any person, any study."

1873–1876

Vanderbilt & Johns Hopkins Founded

Vanderbilt (1873) was endowed by Cornelius Vanderbilt to bring world-class education to the South. Johns Hopkins (1876) became the first American university modeled on the German research university — transforming graduate education nationwide.

1885

Stanford University Founded

Founded by Leland and Jane Stanford in memory of their son, Stanford opened in 1891 on a vast California land grant — and went on to birth the technology ecosystem that became Silicon Valley.

1891–1912

Caltech & Rice University Founded

Caltech (1891) was founded in Pasadena to advance science and engineering on the West Coast; it went on to manage NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory. Rice (1912) opened tuition-free in Houston with a mission of accessible excellence.
