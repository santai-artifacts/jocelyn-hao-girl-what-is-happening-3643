What Is the Ivy League?

More Than an Athletic Conference

The Ivy League began in 1954 as a collegiate athletics conference — but the eight schools it united had been shaping American intellectual life for centuries before that.

Today the term evokes academic rigor, cultural influence, and a network of alumni who have led governments, sciences, and industries across the globe.

History

Four Centuries of Founding

The Ivy League schools were founded across 229 years — each reflecting the era, values, and ambitions of its time.

1636

Harvard University Founded

Established by the Massachusetts Bay Colony — the first institution of higher education in the American colonies, named after its first benefactor, Reverend John Harvard.

1701

Yale University Founded

Chartered in Connecticut as the Collegiate School, later renamed after Elihu Yale whose gift helped fund the college's first permanent home in New Haven.

1746

Princeton University Founded

Originally the College of New Jersey, Princeton was the fourth college established in colonial America and became a center of Presbyterian learning and later secular scholarship.

1754

Columbia University Founded

Founded as King's College by royal charter of King George II, Columbia was established in lower Manhattan and today remains New York City's oldest university.

1755

University of Pennsylvania Founded

Founded by Benjamin Franklin with a uniquely practical mission — to prepare students for lives in business and public service, not just the clergy.

1764

Brown University Founded

Established in Warren, Rhode Island, as the College of Rhode Island. Brown was notably the first U.S. college to accept students regardless of religious affiliation.

1769

Dartmouth College Founded

Originally chartered to educate Native American youth, Dartmouth was founded by Eleazar Wheelock and is the only Ivy League institution designated as a college rather than a university.

1865

Cornell University Founded

Founded by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White on a then-radical idea: "an institution where any person can find instruction in any study" — Ithaca, New York's land-grant university.
